Introduction
In today’s world, blockchain technology has emerged as a revolutionary innovation, offering
secure, transparent, and decentralized solutions for various industries. Cryptocurrencies, built on
blockchain technology, have transformed the way people think about financial systems, providing
faster, cheaper, and more secure methods of transactions. This project, named AFCoin, aims to
create a unique cryptocurrency token based on the ERC-20 standard, combined with innovative
features to enhance user accessibility, engagement, and convenience.
The main goal of the AFCoin project is to design and develop a secure blockchain-based
infrastructure that makes transactions fast, easy, and reliable while keeping costs low.
Additionally, the project introduces a user-friendly website for managing features like Initial
Coin Offerings (ICO), account management, mining using a unique Proof of Knowledge
(PoK) method, and providing detailed information about the token and its development team. The
project ensures that the cryptocurrency is aligned with local financial contexts by setting its value
against the Afghani fiat currency.
1.1 Cryptocurrency in Afghanistan
In Afghanistan, blockchain and cryptocurrency remain relatively unexplored fields. Despite the
rapid adoption of cryptocurrencies worldwide, there has been little to no promotion, development,
or formal use of blockchain technology within the country. Most people are unfamiliar with the
concept of decentralized finance, and there are significant gaps in understanding how blockchain
can be leveraged for secure and transparent transactions.
AFCoin seeks to address this gap by introducing an accessible and practical cryptocurrency
tailored to local needs. By linking its value to the Afghani fiat currency and incorporating easy-touse features, AFCoin aims to educate and involve Afghan users in the global blockchain
revolution. This project could act as a stepping stone for further exploration of blockchain
applications in Afghanistan, fostering innovation in the financial sector and beyond.

2.1 Overview of Blockchain Technology
Blockchain technology is one of the most revolutionary innovations of the 21st century, reshaping
how data is stored, shared, and trusted. It is a distributed ledger system that records transactions
across multiple nodes in a network, ensuring transparency, security, and immutability. Originally
conceived as the backbone of cryptocurrency systems, blockchain has since evolved to serve
countless applications across industries.
The Origins of Blockchain
The concept of blockchain was first introduced in 2008 by an individual or group of
individuals using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto. It was outlined in the whitepaper
titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System", which proposed a
decentralized digital currency system, Bitcoin. This groundbreaking idea sought to
eliminate the need for intermediaries, such as banks, by enabling peer-to-peer
transactions. The first blockchain was implemented in 2009 as the foundation of
Bitcoin, serving as a public ledger for all Bitcoin transactions.
Why Blockchain Was Created
Blockchain emerged as a response to several challenges in traditional financial and data systems.
The financial crisis of 2008 highlighted the risks of centralized financial institutions and the lack
of transparency in global markets. Blockchain aimed to address these issues by creating a
decentralized and transparent system where no single entity had control over the entire network.
Its design ensures trust through cryptography, rather than relying on intermediaries or centralized
authorities.
The primary goals of blockchain were to:
 Enable secure, tamper-proof record-keeping.
 Foster transparency in transactions.
 Reduce dependency on centralized institutions.
 Provide an efficient, low-cost system for transferring value.
How Blockchain Works
At its core, blockchain is a chain of blocks, where each block contains a list of transactions. Each
block is linked to the previous one through a cryptographic hash, creating a secure and
chronological chain. Key features of blockchain include:7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

1. Decentralization: Unlike traditional databases managed by a central authority, blockchain
data is distributed across a network of nodes.
2. Immutability: Once a transaction is recorded, it cannot be altered without consensus from
the majority of the network, ensuring data integrity.
3. Transparency: Public blockchains, such as Bitcoin and Ethereum, allow anyone to view
transactions, fostering trust.
4. Security: Advanced cryptographic algorithms secure the network and protect user data.
The Evolution of Blockchain
While blockchain started as a means to power Bitcoin, its potential was soon recognized beyond
cryptocurrency. In 2015, Ethereum, developed by Vitalik Buterin and a team of contributors,
introduced the concept of smart contracts. These self-executing contracts expanded blockchain's
use cases to include decentralized applications (DApps) in finance, supply chain, healthcare, and
more.
The technology has since matured, with advancements such as:
 Consensus Mechanisms: Beyond Proof of Work (PoW), new mechanisms like Proof of
Stake (PoS) and Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS) have emerged, offering more energyefficient solutions.
 Interoperability: Efforts like Polkadot and Cosmos enable communication between
different blockchain networks, solving the problem of fragmentation.
 Scalability: Layer 2 solutions like Lightning Network and sidechains enhance blockchain's
transaction capacity.

Despite its advantages, blockchain faces challenges like scalability, regulatory uncertainty, and

2.2 Consensus Mechanisms (PoW, PoS)
Blockchain technology relies on a unique concept called consensus mechanisms to ensure that all
participants in a decentralized network agree on the state of the ledger. In simple terms, a consensus
mechanism is a set of rules or protocols that enable nodes in a distributed system to validate
transactions, add new blocks to the blockchain, and maintain network integrity.
In the absence of a central authority, blockchain networks need a reliable way to achieve agreement
among participants. Consensus mechanisms solve critical challenges like:
 Trust: Ensuring data integrity without relying on intermediaries.
 Security: Protecting the network from malicious actors.
 Decentralization: Maintaining equal authority among all nodes.
Different consensus mechanisms address these goals using varying methods, each with its
strengths and weaknesses.
Proof of Work (PoW)
Proof of Work (PoW) is the original consensus mechanism, first implemented in Bitcoin by Satoshi
Nakamoto. It requires participants (miners) to solve complex mathematical problems to validate
transactions and add new blocks to the blockchain.
How It Works:
1. Miners compete to solve a cryptographic puzzle.
2. The first miner to solve the puzzle broadcasts the solution to the network.
3. Other nodes verify the solution. If valid, the new block is added to the blockchain.
4. The miner receives a reward (cryptocurrency) for their work.
Advantages:
 Security: PoW is highly secure due to the computational power required to attack the
network.
 Decentralization: It discourages central control by making mining computationally
expensive.
Disadvantages:
 Energy-Intensive: PoW requires significant energy, raising environmental concerns.
 Scalability Issues: It can process a limited number of transactions per second.
 Hardware Costs: Mining requires expensive hardware, creating barriers to entry7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

Proof of Stake (PoS)
Introduction:
Proof of Stake (PoS) was proposed as an alternative to PoW to address its inefficiencies. Instead
of relying on computational work, PoS selects validators based on the number of coins they hold
and are willing to "stake" as collateral.
How It Works:
1. Validators are chosen randomly to create new blocks and validate transactions.
2. The probability of being selected is proportional to the amount of cryptocurrency staked.
3. Validators earn rewards for their work, but they risk losing their stake if they act
maliciously.
Advantages:
 Energy Efficiency: PoS significantly reduces energy consumption compared to PoW.
 Lower Entry Barriers: No need for specialized mining hardware.
 Scalability: PoS enables faster transaction processing, making it more suitable for highvolume applications.
Disadvantages:
 Centralization Risk: Wealthy participants can dominate the network, as those with more
coins have higher chances of being selected.
 Security Concerns: PoS is less tested than PoW and may be vulnerable to new attack
vectors.
Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS) are two of the most prominent consensus
mechanisms in blockchain technology. PoW, pioneered by Bitcoin in 2009, relies on
computational power to secure the network. Miners solve complex mathematical puzzles to
validate transactions and add new blocks to the blockchain. This process demands substantial
energy consumption and specialized hardware, which has raised environmental and scalability
concerns. However, PoW remains one of the most secure and decentralized mechanisms due to its
reliance on distributed computational efforts.
In contrast, PoS, introduced in projects like Ethereum 2.0, shifts the focus from computational
power to ownership of the cryptocurrency. Validators are chosen based on the number of tokens
they stake, meaning they lock their assets as collateral to propose and validate new blocks. This
approach significantly reduces energy consumption and allows for faster transaction processing.
PoS also offers economic incentives for good behavior, as validators risk losing their staked tokens
if they act maliciously.7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

environmental sustainability. Each mechanism has its advantages and trade-offs, with PoW being
more battle-tested and PoS aligning better with modern concerns like energy efficiency and

blockchain project.

2.3 Overview of ERC-20 Token Standards
The ERC-20 token standard is a widely recognized and implemented protocol in blockchain
ecosystems, particularly on the Ethereum network. It provides a blueprint for creating fungible
tokens—digital assets with uniform properties that are interchangeable, such as cryptocurrencies

What is ERC-20?
ERC-20 stands for "Ethereum Request for Comment 20." It was proposed by Fabian Vogelsteller
in 2015 as an Ethereum Improvement Proposal (EIP-20). This standardizes how tokens operate
within the Ethereum ecosystem, enabling developers to build decentralized applications (DApps)
and smart contracts that interact seamlessly with the tokens. ERC-20 essentially outlines a set of
rules and functions that a token contract must implement to ensure compatibility with other
Ethereum-based tools, such as wallets and exchanges.
Why ERC-20?
Before ERC-20, there was no uniformity in how tokens behaved, which led to inefficiencies and
compatibility issues between DApps, wallets, and tokens. ERC-20 addressed this problem by
offering a common standard that all developers could adopt. This uniformity has been instrumental
in the rise of Initial Coin Offerings (ICOs), where projects raise funds by issuing their ERC-20
tokens.
Key Features of ERC-20
The ERC-20 standard includes the following six mandatory functions and three optional ones:
1. Mandatory Functions:
o totalSupply: Specifies the total number of tokens in circulation.
o balanceOf(address owner): Returns the balance of tokens for a given address.
o transfer(address to, uint256 value): Allows users to send tokens directly to
another address.
o transferFrom(address from, address to, uint256 value): Enables automated
transfers under certain conditions (e.g., escrow or subscriptions).
o approve(address spender, uint256 value): Allows an address to spend a specified
amount of tokens on behalf of the owner.
o allowance(address owner, address spender): Checks the remaining tokens an
approved spender can use.
2. Optional Functions:
o name: Name of the token (e.g., "MyToken").
o symbol: Short representation of the token (e.g., "MTK").
o decimals: Specifies the divisibility of the token (e.g., 18 decimals).
Benefits of ERC-20
 Interoperability: ERC-20 tokens are compatible with Ethereum wallets, DApps, and
exchanges, ensuring broad usability.
 Ease of Deployment: Developers can deploy tokens quickly using predefined rules.
 Security: The standardized design reduces bugs and vulnerabilities.7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

Limitations of ERC-20
While ERC-20 has revolutionized token creation, it is not without drawbacks. Issues like gas
inefficiency, token stuck in contracts, and the inability to handle certain edge cases have led to the
development of more advanced standards like ERC-777.
The ERC-20 token standard has revolutionized the blockchain ecosystem by offering a unified
framework for creating and managing fungible tokens. Its simplicity, interoperability, and
widespread adoption have enabled seamless integration across wallets, exchanges, and
decentralized applications. While it has some limitations, such as gas inefficiency and handling
errors, ERC-20 remains the cornerstone of token creation on the Ethereum network. The standard
has catalyzed innovations like Initial Coin Offerings (ICOs), empowering developers and
entrepreneurs to launch decentralized projects efficiently. As blockchain technology evolves,
ERC-20 will continue to play a pivotal role in fostering innovation and accessibility.
2.4 Tokenomics and Pricing Models
Tokenomics, a combination of "token" and "economics," refers to the economic model of a
cryptocurrency or token. It involves designing, analyzing, and understanding the supply and
demand dynamics of a token within a blockchain ecosystem. The success of a cryptocurrency often
hinges on its tokenomics, which influences user adoption, utility, and value appreciation.
What is Tokenomics?
Tokenomics encompasses several elements, including the token's use case, supply mechanisms,
distribution strategies, and governance structures. It aims to align incentives among stakeholders,
such as users, investors, developers, and platform operators.
1. Utility: The primary driver of a token's value is its utility. Tokens can represent:
o Currency: Medium of exchange (e.g., Bitcoin).
o Access: Entry to specific services or platforms.
o Rewards: Incentivizing participation in networks or ecosystems.
o Governance: Voting rights in decentralized decision-making.
2. Supply Mechanisms: Tokenomics defines how tokens are created and distributed.
o Fixed Supply: Tokens are limited in quantity (e.g., Bitcoin's cap of 21 million).
o Inflationary Supply: Tokens are continuously minted, often with a fixed inflation
rate.
o Burn Mechanisms: Tokens are periodically removed from circulation to reduce
supply and increase scarcity.
3. Distribution Strategies: The initial and ongoing distribution of tokens impacts their value
and adoption.
o Initial Coin Offerings (ICOs): Raising funds by selling tokens to investors.
o Airdrops: Distributing free tokens to promote adoption.
o Mining/Staking Rewards: Rewarding users for network participation.7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

Pricing Models for Tokens
The price of a token is influenced by a combination of market dynamics, tokenomics, and external
factors such as regulatory environments. Below are some common pricing models:
1. Market-Driven Pricing: Token prices are determined by supply and demand on
exchanges. Speculative trading often leads to high volatility.
2. Utility-Based Pricing: The intrinsic value of a token is derived from its utility within an
ecosystem. For instance, a token enabling access to premium services will be valued based
on demand for those services.
3. Network Valuation Models:
o Metcalfe’s Law: The value of a network is proportional to the square of its number
of active users.
o NVT Ratio: Compares a network’s value (market cap) to its transaction volume to
assess whether a token is overvalued or undervalued.
4. Auction-Based Models: Used during ICOs or Initial Exchange Offerings (IEOs), where
tokens are sold at a price determined by bidder demand.
5. Fiat Conversion Models: Particularly relevant in contexts like Afghanistan, where users
may need clear mechanisms to convert tokens into local currency (Afghani).
Importance of Tokenomics and Pricing Models
A well-thought-out tokenomics strategy ensures the long-term sustainability of a cryptocurrency
project. It attracts investors, fosters user engagement, and creates a balanced ecosystem where
supply and demand dynamics maintain token value. Conversely, poor tokenomics can lead to
issues like inflation, loss of user trust, and project failure.
Tokenomics is the backbone of a successful cryptocurrency or token-based project, influencing its
utility, adoption, and market performance. By carefully designing supply mechanisms, utility
functions, and distribution strategies, developers can create ecosystems that sustain long-term
growth and user engagement. Similarly, robust pricing models, whether driven by utility, market
dynamics, or network valuation metrics, are critical for maintaining token value and investor
confidence. Effective tokenomics aligns incentives across stakeholders, creating a balanced and
thriving ecosystem. In a competitive and evolving blockchain landscape, projects with strong
tokenomics are best positioned to achieve sustainability and success.
ERC-20 token standards and robust tokenomics are foundational pillars of modern cryptocurrency
ecosystems. While ERC-20 provides a technical framework for creating interoperable tokens,
tokenomics ensures that these tokens are valuable, sustainable, and aligned with stakeholder goals.
Both are essential for driving innovation and adoption in the blockchain space.7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

3.1 Blockchain Infrastructure and Security
Blockchain infrastructure refers to the underlying architecture and systems that enable blockchain
networks to operate. It is a distributed ledger technology (DLT) that ensures transparency,
immutability, and decentralization. The infrastructure is composed of nodes, consensus
algorithms, cryptographic techniques, and data storage mechanisms. Each blockchain network,
whether public like Bitcoin and Ethereum or private like Hyperledger Fabric, has its unique
infrastructure tailored to its use case.
At the heart of blockchain infrastructure are nodes, which are individual computers or servers that
store copies of the blockchain and participate in its maintenance. Nodes validate transactions, add
them to the ledger, and ensure data consistency across the network. Blockchain’s distributed nature
eliminates the need for a central authority, making it resilient to tampering and censorship.
Key Components of Blockchain Infrastructure
1. Nodes:
o Nodes can be full, light, or validating nodes, each with different roles in the
network. Full nodes store the entire blockchain and validate new transactions, while
light nodes rely on full nodes for specific data.
o Example: Bitcoin full nodes maintain the entire transaction history, ensuring
transparency.
2. Consensus Mechanisms:
o Consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work (PoW), Proof of Stake (PoS), and
others enable decentralized agreement on transaction validity.
o Example: Bitcoin uses PoW, while Ethereum has transitioned to PoS to improve
energy efficiency.
3. Cryptographic Techniques:
o Blockchain relies on cryptographic hashing, digital signatures, and encryption to
secure data.
o Example: SHA-256 is the hashing algorithm used in Bitcoin, ensuring data
integrity.
4. Smart Contracts:
o These are self-executing agreements written in code, stored on the blockchain.
o Example: Ethereum supports smart contracts, enabling DApps like decentralized
finance (DeFi).

Blockchain Security
Blockchain security is paramount to ensure the integrity and trustworthiness of decentralized
systems. Its security is rooted in cryptographic principles, decentralization, and consensus
mechanisms.

require immense computational resources, making tampering nearly impossible.
o Example: The Bitcoin blockchain has never been successfully altered since its
inception.

if some nodes are compromised, the network remains operational.
3. Consensus Protocols:

network integrity.
o Example: Ethereum's PoS discourages attacks by requiring validators to stake
cryptocurrency.
4. Encryption and Privacy:
o Public blockchains use encryption to protect user data, while private blockchains
can implement additional access controls.

3.3 ERC-20 Token Implementation Principles
ERC-20 is a technical standard used for creating and implementing tokens on the Ethereum

Introduced in 2015, ERC-20 has become the most widely adopted standard for token creation on

decentralized finance (DeFi) and initial coin offerings (ICOs).

4.2 Transaction Processing and Cost Analysis
Reducing Transaction Costs and Gas Fees on the Ethereum Blockchain
One of the most significant challenges facing the Ethereum blockchain is the high transaction
costs, also known as gas fees. Gas fees can fluctuate based on network demand, and high fees can
be a barrier for users, especially in decentralized finance (DeFi) applications.
Here are some methods to we could find to reduce transaction costs on Ethereum:
1. Layer 2 Solutions: Ethereum’s Layer 2 solutions, such as Optimistic Rollups and zkRollups, allow for faster and cheaper transactions by processing transactions off-chain and
then posting the results back to the Ethereum mainnet. This helps in reducing congestion
on the main chain and lowering transaction fees.
2. Batching Transactions: Instead of submitting multiple transactions individually, batching
transactions together can significantly reduce the overall transaction cost. This is especially
useful for decentralized applications (dApps) that need to process many small transactions.
3. Gas Token Optimization: Gas tokens, like CHI and GST2, can be used to reduce the
effective gas cost by "minting" gas during low network congestion and redeeming it during
high congestion. This allows users to effectively manage their transaction costs.
4. Transaction Fee Estimations and Optimization: By using gas estimators, dApps can
dynamically adjust transaction fees based on real-time network conditions. Users can set
limits on the amount of gas they are willing to spend, and the system will automatically
optimize the transaction to use the least possible gas.
5. Adoption of Proof of Stake (PoS): The transition of Ethereum from PoW to PoS (which
is underway with Ethereum 2.0) is expected to reduce gas fees by improving network
scalability. PoS will allow Ethereum to process more transactions per second (TPS),
thereby decreasing congestion and transaction fees.
By implementing these strategies, AFCoin can improve user experience by lowering transaction
costs on the Ethereum network and making interactions more affordable.

Appendix A: Glossary
1. Blockchain
A decentralized distributed ledger that records transactions across multiple nodes using
cryptography, ensuring data integrity and immutability. It forms the backbone of
cryptocurrencies, enabling transparent and secure peer-to-peer transactions.
2. Cryptocurrency
A form of digital or virtual currency that uses cryptography for secure transactions and
operates on decentralized networks, typically based on blockchain technology. Examples
include Bitcoin, Ethereum, and AFCoin.
3. Web3
The decentralized internet framework that allows users to control their own data and
digital identities through blockchain, smart contracts, and decentralized applications
(dApps). Web3 aims to replace centralized intermediaries with peer-to-peer systems.
4. Decentralized Application (dApp)
An application that runs on a blockchain or decentralized network, rather than centralized
servers. It is self-executing and governed by smart contracts, ensuring transparency,
security, and user control.
5. Ethereum
A decentralized blockchain platform supporting smart contracts and dApps, facilitating
peer-to-peer transactions through its native cryptocurrency, Ether (ETH). Ethereum
enables decentralized finance (DeFi) and token creation.
6. Smart Contract
A self-executing contract with the terms directly encoded as executable code on a
blockchain. It automatically enforces and executes contractual agreements without the
need for intermediaries, enhancing trust and reducing risk.
7. ERC-20 Token Standard
A technical standard for creating fungible tokens on the Ethereum blockchain. ERC-20
defines essential functions like transfer, approve, and allowance, ensuring
interoperability with Ethereum-based wallets and exchanges.
8. MetaMask
A browser extension and mobile wallet that enables users to interact with Ethereumbased applications and manage Ether (ETH) and ERC-20 tokens. MetaMask acts as a
gateway to the decentralized web.
9. Solidity
A contract-oriented programming language used to write smart contracts for the
Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM). Solidity enables the development of decentralized
applications and is designed for security, efficiency, and scalability.
10. Hardhat
A development framework for Ethereum applications, enabling developers to compile,
test, and deploy smart contracts. Hardhat provides a local Ethereum network for testing,
simplifying the development process.
11. Web3.js
A JavaScript library that provides an interface to interact with the Ethereum blockchain.
It allows developers to send transactions, query data, and invoke smart contract functions
within decentralized applications.7th Semester Work Report For Cryptocurrency(AFCoin)

12. Ether.js
A JavaScript library for interacting with the Ethereum blockchain, emphasizing
simplicity and security. Ether.js facilitates wallet management, signing transactions, and
interacting with smart contracts in dApps.
13. Initial Coin Offering (ICO)
A crowdfunding method in which new cryptocurrency projects sell tokens to investors in
exchange for capital. ICOs often offer early-stage access to tokens at discounted prices
before they are listed on exchanges.
14. Tokenomics
The study of the economic aspects of cryptocurrency tokens, including their supply,
demand, utility, and governance. Tokenomics defines how a token behaves in its
ecosystem, influencing its value and market dynamics.
15. Gas Fee
The fee paid to miners or validators to process transactions and execute smart contracts
on the Ethereum network. Gas fees are typically denominated in Ether (ETH) and vary
based on network congestion and transaction complexity.
16. Mining
The process of validating and adding new transactions to the blockchain in Proof of Work
(PoW) systems. Miners solve complex mathematical problems to secure the network,
earning rewards in the form of cryptocurrency.
17. Burning (Token Burn)
The act of permanently removing a set number of tokens from circulation by sending
them to a designated burn address, reducing the total supply to potentially increase
scarcity and value.
18. Allowance
A feature in ERC-20 tokens that specifies the amount of tokens a spender is authorized to
transfer from the token owner’s balance. Allowances prevent unauthorized transfers and
ensure that spending limits are respected.
19. Approval
A function in the ERC-20 standard allowing token owners to authorize other addresses to
transfer a set number of tokens on their behalf. This ensures control over token
expenditure and enables token-based delegations.
20. TransferFrom
An ERC-20 function enabling approved spenders to transfer tokens on behalf of the token
holder. It requires prior approval to ensure that only authorized entities can execute
transfers.
21. Freeze
A mechanism in token contracts that can temporarily or permanently halt transactions or
transfers of specific tokens or addresses. Freezing may be implemented for security,
governance, or regulatory reasons.
22. Meta-Transaction
A transaction executed on behalf of a user by a third party (relayer), enabling users to
interact with dApps without directly paying gas fees. The relayer submits the transaction
and covers the gas costs.
23. Transaction History

24. OpenZeppelin

decentralized applications. Web3 wallets like MetaMask and Trust Wallet allow users to
